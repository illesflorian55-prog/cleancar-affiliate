## Why School and Work Networks Can Block ChatGPT

When you connect to a Wi-Fi network at school or work, your internet traffic flows through their routers and servers. The network administrator uses firewall software to monitor and filter this traffic:
1. They maintain a blacklist of domain names (like `chatgpt.com` or `openai.com`).
2. When your browser requests to load one of these domains, the router detects the destination.
3. The router intercepts the request and displays a block page instead of the actual website.

Because they see exactly which domains you are trying to visit, they can selectively block anything they choose.

A VPN completely bypasses this logic by **encrypting your traffic**. When you turn on a VPN, all data leaving your device is scrambled into unreadable code. The school or office router can only see that you are connected to a VPN server, but it has no idea what website you are actually visiting. Because the router cannot see that you are loading `chatgpt.com`, it cannot block it!

## What to Do If Your VPN is Blocked

Some highly restrictive networks block known VPN server IP addresses or block the VPN protocols themselves. If you run into issues, try these quick fixes:

* **Use Obfuscated Servers:** Go to your VPN app's advanced settings and enable obfuscation (or "Stealth" mode). This makes your VPN traffic look like normal secure web browsing (HTTPS).
* **Change the Protocol:** Try switching from OpenVPN to WireGuard or IKEv2. Sometimes, a network blocks one protocol but forgets to block others.
* **Try the Browser Extension:** If the desktop application fails to connect, the lightweight browser extension will often succeed because it operates solely within the browser's sandbox.
