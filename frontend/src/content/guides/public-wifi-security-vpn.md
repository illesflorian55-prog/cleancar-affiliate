## The Illusion of Safety

When you connect to your Wi-Fi at home, your router acts as a bouncer. It requires a password (usually WPA2 or WPA3 encryption) to join. This means the airwaves between your laptop and your router are scrambled. Even if a neighbor intercepts the signal, all they see is mathematical gibberish.

Public Wi-Fi networks, especially the ones that don't require a password, lack this fundamental layer of encryption. They are "open" networks. 

When you send an email, enter a password, or load a webpage on an open network, that data is broadcast in plain text through the air for anyone nearby to grab. ## The Hacker's Toolkit: How They Steal Your Data

You don't need to be a sophisticated cybercriminal mastermind in a dark hoodie to exploit a public Wi-Fi network. The tools required to intercept your data are shockingly accessible, free to download, and can be run by a teenager on a standard laptop. 

Here are the three most common traps laid on public networks:

### 1. The Man-in-the-Middle (MitM) Attack
This is the classic public Wi-Fi exploit. In a MitM attack, the hacker secretly positions their computer between your device and the router.
